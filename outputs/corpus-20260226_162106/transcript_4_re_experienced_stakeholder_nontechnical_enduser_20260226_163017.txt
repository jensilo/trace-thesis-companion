Interviewer: Thanks for meeting with me. To start, can you tell me a bit about your role and a typical day handling disputes?

Stakeholder: Sure — I’m on the cardmember services team. Interviewer: Walk me through a single case from when it first lands to when you close or escalate it. What do you do step by step?

Stakeholder: Okay, so a case comes in — either through a phone call or a ticket. I try to find the transaction, check the statement line to make sure the amount and date match, then look at the member record to confirm identity and contact info, and then I look up the merchant to see if we’ve handled anything like this before. If it’s straightforward I open a dispute or send a retrieval request; if not, I escalate to an investigator. Along the way I jot notes, attach any screenshots, and sometimes I have to wait on another team to confirm something before I can proceed.

Interviewer: Which systems are you opening today to do those checks? Can you name them for me — what you rely on now?

Stakeholder: I jump into our statement look-up, the member profile system, and a merchant directory — plus our current ticketing tool. I also sometimes keep a spreadsheet open where I track odd things; don’t tell IT, but yeah, that’s a messy workaround.

Interviewer: You mentioned juggling several cases at once. How many do you commonly have open in parallel, when things are busy?

Stakeholder: Hmm, on a busy morning I’d say... enough that I wish the UI let me tab through them faster — maybe a handful, sometimes more if there’s a wave of chargebacks. I can’t give you an exact number off the top of my head, but I definitely have multiple active ones.

Interviewer: Understood. Thanks — that helps. You said “it’s a pain” switching between systems. When you think about a new Disputes application, what would make that feel better day-to-day?

Stakeholder: It needs to feel familiar. If it looks and behaves like our other company apps, I don’t have to hunt for buttons or settings. Also, putting the key info — transaction details, member contact, merchant notes — on the same screen would stop so much clicking around. And, I want to be confident that what I’m seeing is the official info, not some stale copy someone exported last week.

Interviewer: When you say “familiar,” which company apps work well for you? What exactly about their look-and-feel helps?

Stakeholder: The ones with the standard header, the same search box up top, and where actions are in the same place — like a toolbar with Create, Save, Escalate — that consistency is big. Also, fonts and spacing that don’t feel cramped. If the new app follows our corporate look, new hires pick it up faster. And, uh, when I’m on the phone with a cardmember I don’t want to see internal-only jargon or controls — that confuses people.

Interviewer: Good point about external views. So for screens you might share or show to a member or merchant, what must be different compared to internal views?

Stakeholder: Basically, anything that’s internal workflow — like “investigator notes” or internal escalation flags — should be hidden or masked. Externally visible items should be clean and plain-language, so I don’t blurt out something we’re not supposed to. Oh, and it should still be consistent with our corporate UI even when external — don’t reinvent the layout.

Interviewer: You mentioned transaction details and member info needing to be “official.” Can you say more about what you mean by that? Where should the app get the data from?

Stakeholder: I mean, if the app shows a transaction amount or a member’s address, that needs to be pulled from the master systems we already use — the statement records, the member database, and the merchant directory. If it shows something else, I’ll waste time chasing a phantom error. So the screen has to be tied to those source systems, not some copy someone pasted.

Interviewer: Which specific fields do you rely on coming from each source — for example, the statement source versus the member database versus merchant info? Can you give examples?

Stakeholder: Sure — the transaction date, amount, auth code and the statement line should be from the statement records. The member’s name, contact numbers, address and account status should come from the member database. The merchant name, location, and merchant category — that’s from the merchant directory. If those are wrong, my whole case is shaky.

Interviewer: If the authoritative system is temporarily down, what would you prefer the app do — show something stale, block you, or give a warning? How should it behave?

Stakeholder: I’d want a clear warning. It can show cached info if necessary, but it must say it’s cached and timestamp it so I know when it was last verified. I don’t want to assume data is fresh when it isn’t. And if it’s critical, I want to be able to call it out to a supervisor, not accidentally proceed.

Interviewer: You raised caching and freshness — that’s important. Let’s talk about the list view where you search and see results. You said earlier you want the key info on one screen — how do you want the list of cases to look when you search?

Stakeholder: When I search, I get a bunch of results and right now I have to open each one to know its status. I’d like color flags so I can scan and spot urgent ones. But, uh, I’m colorblind so colors alone won’t cut it — there should be icons or short labels too. And hovering over a result should give me a quick preview so I can decide which to open.

Interviewer: Which statuses are most important to surface in that list? Can you tell me which ones you’d want prioritized?

Stakeholder: The ones I check first are those needing immediate action, ones waiting on member info, and ones that are already resolved or closed. The urgent ones are the biggest pain because they jump to the front of my day. I’d like them to stand out, then the “pending info” ones, then the quiet or closed ones.

Interviewer: When you say “stand out,” what would you expect to happen when you interact with a colored item — do you want a tooltip, a quick preview, or to go straight to the full case?

Stakeholder: A quick preview first would be great, maybe the most recent notes and the primary transaction details, and then I can open the full case if I need to. Tooltips are fine too, but sometimes I need more context than a tiny hover can give.

Interviewer: You mentioned accessibility — since you’re colorblind, do you prefer specific icons or text labels along with colors?

Stakeholder: Definitely both. Icons that are distinct — like an exclamation for urgent — plus a small text label like “Urgent” or “Pending Info.” That way I don’t have to squint or guess.

Interviewer: Let’s talk about who can do what. Today, can everyone initiate a dispute, or is that limited?

Stakeholder: Not everyone should. Right now some people try to open disputes who aren’t assigned to the same cardholder group, and then it becomes a mess. We have to call a supervisor to get it opened for them. I think only certain roles should start a dispute — reps handling those accounts, supervisors, and maybe investigators — but, uh, supervisors sometimes need to act across different account pools. That should be controlled and logged.

Interviewer: When you say “cardholder group” or “cardholder base,” how is that defined in your organization — by region, product, BIN, or something else?

Stakeholder: It’s mostly by assigned portfolios and product lines, and sometimes by BIN ranges for certain cards. Managers assign the account ownership when people are onboarded, and that determines which members we can service.

Interviewer: If a supervisor or investigator needs cross-base access occasionally, how should that be handled from your perspective? Logged, approved in advance, or something else?

Stakeholder: Logged for sure. Ideally there’s an exception workflow — like a request button that sends a note and supervisor approval, and every override is recorded with who did it and why. We get audited sometimes, so I don’t want someone randomly poking into other people’s cases without a trail.

Interviewer: Good — logging is important. Speaking of trails, what kind of audit information do you expect for each case?

Stakeholder: I need to see who opened the case, who viewed it, what they changed, and timestamps for each action. If notes were edited, show that too. If something was escalated or an override happened, that has to be in the record so we can explain our steps to a member or an auditor.

Interviewer: That makes sense. Now, performance — you used words like “fast” and “I don’t want to wait.” Can you put a number on “fast” for me? How long is too long when you move from one screen to another?

Stakeholder: Hmm, numbers aren’t my thing, but, um, when it’s a small move I can tell after a few seconds if it’s lagging. Maybe... five seconds feels like the outer limit before it’s annoying. If it’s longer than that I get distracted and sometimes forget what I was doing.

Interviewer: Is that five seconds acceptable for all transitions, or are there some you expect to be faster or can tolerate a bit longer?

Stakeholder: For small things like opening a case preview or switching between tabs, I’d want it quicker — ideally faster than that. For big things like loading a full transaction history, a little longer is okay, but the UI should show progress so I know it’s working.

Stakeholder: Yes, we have people in different time zones and chargebacks come in anytime. We need it most of the time, every day. Stakeholder: Read-only would be helpful. If I can at least pull up a case I was working on and copy notes to finish later, that’s better than getting booted out.

Stakeholder: Absolutely. If I can pick a template for a retrieval or dispute letter and the system fills in the member name, transaction, and reason, that would save a ton. Also, having standard responses helps keep us compliant.

Stakeholder: We have a set of required language for certain letters and time frames for responses. The app should use the approved templates so we don’t accidentally send something that gets us in trouble. And like I said, every sent letter should be logged on the case.

Stakeholder: Ideally request-based with quick approval. A supervisor could approve access for a user for a specific case or time window, and that action and reason are recorded. That balances security and operational needs.

Stakeholder: Right, so if the corporate UI uses color coding elsewhere, we should follow the same palette, but always include icons and labels so people like me aren’t stuck guessing. Also make sure contrast is high enough and that there are keyboard shortcuts — I find those speed things up.

Stakeholder: First, put the key data on one screen so we stop jumping between tools. Second, the role and base restrictions — I really don’t want to see cases that aren’t mine. Third, that color-coded, accessible list view with previews. Everything else is important, but those three would make the biggest day-to-day difference.
