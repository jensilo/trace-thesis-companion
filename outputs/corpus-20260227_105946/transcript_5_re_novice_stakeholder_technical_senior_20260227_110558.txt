Stakeholder: Mostly claims adjusters and collision estimators, both in-office and in the field. There's also going to be oversight people — auditors — who will poke into estimates, and program administrators who will manage configurations. Field users need a clean, quick interface; office users might do deeper analysis. I should say, not everyone should see everything — some data is sensitive and only admins should have the broad view.

Stakeholder: Yes, absolutely. We can't reinvent the stack; we need to align with the corporate architecture patterns and the UI standards. That's not negotiable. Stakeholder: There's an existing recycled parts provider we use — it's the company's canonical feed for salvaged components. Stakeholder: Yes — to be concrete, our operations group will expect ninety-eight percent availability as the baseline. That implies downtime of about two percent of the time as the maximum tolerated. It's not ideal, but it's what the business has signed off on.

Stakeholder: Server-side caching is the primary lever, with opportunity for short-lived client caching for repeated local lookups.
