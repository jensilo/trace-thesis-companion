Stakeholder: Extremely important. Every action a user takes — opening the case, editing notes, sending a letter, requesting an adjustment — we need to know who did what and when. Stakeholder: They should go to the system that actually posts things to accounts. We can't have the disputes tool directly changing balances; it should hand off a request to the billing/posting system and then record the outcome. That way the ledger stays clean and auditable.

Stakeholder: It needs to be available basically whenever reps are working and also for some external users. We can't have it down during the day. We tolerate some planned early-morning maintenance, but otherwise it should be up most of the time.

Stakeholder: Hmm, I think we typically schedule maintenance in the very early hours. For most days it's a short window overnight, and Sunday's window is a bit longer for heavier jobs. It's that annoying overnight thing; I can get you the exact times if you need them.

Stakeholder: It just needs to feel snappy. I hate watching spinners; reps get impatient and try to reload things or call over a manager. For small screens and searches it should be almost instant — like, not something that makes you walk away.

Stakeholder: Maybe around five seconds for the small transitions? Less is better, but once you start waiting longer than that it becomes noticeable. I know that's not a hard engineering metric, but that's the user's perception.

Stakeholder: At peak times we have a few hundred people in the system concurrently. It's busier during dispute surges. So whatever we build needs to handle that without getting sluggish, otherwise supervisors will complain.

Stakeholder: Sure — I believe our planning number is three hundred and fifty concurrent active users at peak, but again that can vary if there's a billing incident or a big merchant issue.

Interviewer: Thanks. You mentioned earlier that reps shouldn't have to jump around systems. Which systems should the Disputes app talk to directly?

Stakeholder: It should talk to the places that are the masters for data. So the transaction store for statements, the member information store for cardmember details, and the posting/billing system for adjustments. I want the disputes tool to orchestrate these calls so reps don't have to.

Stakeholder: They exist, yeah. We have teams that own them. There will be some coordination and maybe some wrinkles, but they're the canonical places for the data and the operations.

Stakeholder: Apart from logging every action, we need to make sure the app follows corporate policies for customer data handling and interfaces. Also, the look and feel should align with our corporate UI so external users feel it's the same brand when we expose parts of this to merchants or partners. It's jarring when internal tools look different from customer-facing apps.

Interviewer: So consistent UI for internal and external, got it. Anything about external users — like merchants or cardmembers — using the app directly?

Stakeholder: We might have a portal for merchants or for certain partners, but it'll be limited compared to what reps see. The external view shouldn't expose internal-only data, and it should be simpler. Oh, and we need strong separation so one merchant can't see another merchant's disputes. Same for reps and their cardholder bases.

Interviewer: Right. On data segregation, is the rule "see only your own customers" across the board?

Stakeholder: Yeah, broadly. There are escalation paths where someone temporarily needs access, but that should be controlled and auditable. Day-to-day, people must only see what they're entitled to.

Interviewer: Great. Any usability features reps care about? Like quick search, filters, notes?

Stakeholder: They want quick search, yeah, and the ability to add notes and see the case history inline. A lot of reps prefer a single page where they can do the essentials without clicking too much. Also, having templates for common correspondence is a big time-saver. And, um, a small pet peeve — checks and balances for actions that have financial impact, like a mandatory supervisor approval popup or something, would help avoid mistakes.

Stakeholder: Managers want dashboards for queue health and aging disputes and to see who is overloaded. Reps sometimes want personal queues. I won't pretend I know all the metrics; reporting folks will want standard things like counts, ages, and maybe amounts by dispute reason.

Stakeholder: Plenty. We had an incident where a rep adjusted an account in error because they thought a field was editable; the audit trail was incomplete and it took days to sort out. And sometimes maintenance knocks people out during business hours because windows weren't communicated well. Those kinds of things are the pain points we want to fix.
