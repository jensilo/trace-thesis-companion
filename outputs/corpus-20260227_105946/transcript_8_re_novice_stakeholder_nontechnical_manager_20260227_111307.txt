We want a product that feels welcoming, that sells films reliably, and that gives us the business signals we need to decide what to promote next. Oh, and it should reflect an African aesthetic, but, you know, modern and simple so people don't get lost.

Stakeholder: Well, it should be straightforward. I mean, most people should be able to use it without training. It should feel obvious where to go, how to buy, how to play a movie. I think if someone is used to basic apps or websites, they shouldn't be stuck.

Stakeholder: Signing up should be fast and not annoying. We don't want ten pages of forms or anything like that — just the essentials so people don't drop off mid-signup.

Stakeholder: Hmm, yeah, that's fair to be concrete. I think users should reach an account within about four pages at most. Any more than that and we'll lose a lot of people.

Stakeholder: The flow has to be frictionless. A customer should be able to pick a movie, pay, and start watching with very little clicking or waiting. The moment of truth is being able to actually play the movie quickly after deciding to buy.

Stakeholder: By quick I mean a few minutes — it should be under three minutes from deciding to buy to seeing the first frame, assuming their internet isn't terrible.

Stakeholder: Uh, if I had to pick a number, I'd say roughly five seconds for the usual things, like loading a page or adding to cart. Any longer and people notice and get annoyed.

Stakeholder: Payments are a bit more sensitive. People tolerate a little more time while a card is being processed, but it still needs to be fast enough that folks don't think the payment failed and try again. Slow or failed payments cost us money and trust.

Stakeholder: I think up to around fifteen seconds is acceptable for a card transaction; beyond that we start seeing abandoned attempts or duplicate charges from panicked users.

Stakeholder: Pre-paid cards are important for parts of our audience who prefer cash or who don't use cards. Generating and redeeming those should be simple for admins and for users. Interviewer: You mean like people should still be able to browse and watch? Or buy with pre-paid?

Stakeholder: Right, browsing should definitely continue. Ideally people could redeem pre-paid codes or use balances even if the gateway is down. We want graceful degradation so revenue isn't completely blocked and users don't get the impression the whole business collapsed.

Interviewer: That makes sense. On streaming itself, any expectations about how long users should wait for buffering?

Stakeholder: Streaming has to be smooth. If a person clicks play and watches a spinner forever, they'll leave. Short buffering periods are fine, but it should feel immediate. I think a few seconds is okay, but not much more.

Interviewer: Can you say what "a few seconds" means in practice?

Stakeholder: Sure — I would say buffering should generally finish within about ten seconds, plus whatever lag the user's connection adds. So the system shouldn't introduce the delay.

Interviewer: Okay. How important is uptime and reliability from a business perspective?

Stakeholder: It's very important. Downtime hurts us twice: lost revenue and damaged reputation. When the site is down during a popular release or a weekend, that's money and goodwill gone. We need to be dependable.

Interviewer: Is there a target availability you have in mind?

Stakeholder: Yes, we need it to be up almost all the time. If pressed, our goal is something like ninety-nine and a half percent availability over the year. Anything less and I would worry about the platform's credibility.

Interviewer: Got it. About security — you mentioned payments — who should be able to see customers' payment details?

Stakeholder: Payment details are sensitive. Only a few trusted people should be able to access full card details, and access should be strictly controlled. We also want data to be stored in a way that, even if someone got to it, it wouldn't be trivially usable. It's a compliance and trust thing for customers.

Interviewer: So, limited admin access and encrypted storage?

Stakeholder: Exactly. Authorized Izogn admins only, and the payment information needs to be encrypted in the database. We're not going to have support reps waltzing in and reading card numbers.

Interviewer: Hearing the privacy side, how should we make users aware of our data practices?

Stakeholder: We must be upfront; people should know what we collect and how we use it before they type anything. A clear privacy policy that they can access is important, and it should be obvious — not buried.

Interviewer: Where should that privacy policy be available?

Stakeholder: It should be reachable from anywhere on the site. Ideally a link on every page, so users can check it at any point before they hand over any personal info.

Interviewer: Alright. Switching to admin features for a moment — you mentioned analytics. What do admins need to see?

Stakeholder: Admins need actionable business analytics: what sells, viewing patterns, revenue trends — the usual marketing and operations signals. The dashboards should let us spot a title that's suddenly trending, or notice when purchases drop so we can react.

Stakeholder: Access should be role-based. Not everyone needs the same things; some people need full reports, others just a summary. We don't want broad access to everything.

Stakeholder: The whole flow should be designed around minimal friction. Big buttons, clear labels, minimal form fields, plain language. And, um, a quick checkout option for regulars so returning customers don't have to repeat steps. Also, small tangent here, but we need localized text and payment options for users in different countries — sometimes people prefer mobile money, sometimes pre-paid cards you can buy in a shop. Stakeholder: The system should support issuing and validating codes, and we should be able to bulk-generate cards for partners to sell physically. How those partners distribute them can vary, but the platform must manage the codes and redemptions cleanly.

Interviewer: Okay. Last quick things — any constraints about how many clicks or pages a customer should go through to purchase and start watching, overall?

Stakeholder: The fewer the better. We already said sign-up should be short, and buying-to-play should be under a few minutes. I think customers shouldn't have to hop through a maze of pages — keep it tight and obvious.
